Phase 1: Discover (per opportunity)

Sources to monitor

  • LinkedIn — job feed + Easy Apply filter
  • Greenhouse / Lever direct company pages (often not posted on LinkedIn)
  • Company career pages — bookmark target companies (see companies_target.md)
  • Referrals — your strongest lever; ask your network BEFORE applying cold
  • Communities — Indonesia tech Discord/Telegram groups, r/cscareerquestions

Decision: should I apply?

Filter out before applying: - [ ] Salary range visible and matches your floor? - [ ] Years of experience required ≤ your years? (or within 2 years gap) - [ ] Stack matches ≥ 60% of your skills? - [ ] Location / remote OK for you? - [ ] Company stage = a place you'd actually work? (not just desperate apply)

If ≥3 boxes ticked → apply. If <3 → skip, don't waste cycles.

Phase 2: Tailor (per opportunity, ~10-15 min)

Step-by-step

  1. Save the JD: copy JD text → save to knowledge/jds/<company>_<role>.txt
  2. Parse it: python3 scripts/jd_parser.py --file knowledge/jds/<company>_<role>.txt
  3. Plan tailoring: python3 scripts/tailor_cv.py --file knowledge/jds/<company>_<role>.txt --role sdet
  4. Pick the right CV variant: based on the parser output (Backend/AI/QA/SDET)
  5. Edit the CV (10 min max): swap 2-3 bullets to match JD keywords, update Profile Summary if needed
  6. Save as: CV/<Role>/CV_Noorman_<Role>_<Company>.docx (variant for this specific app)
  7. Personalize cover letter: copy CoverLetter/<Role>/CL_Template_<Role>.docx → fill in:
  8. [Date], [Hiring Manager], [Company Name], [specific reason]
  9. Save as: CL_<Company>_<Role>.docx
  10. Export both to PDF: soffice --headless --convert-to pdf file.docx

When NOT to tailor

  • Easy Apply with no JD-specific required fields → use the role-appropriate CV as-is
  • Volume-based application rounds (you're short on time) → use base CV, batch apply

Phase 3: Submit

Per platform workflow

LinkedIn Easy Apply (see hacking_ways/linkedin_easy_apply.md): - Use Easy Apply filter - Pre-fill answers: years of experience, work authorization, salary expectation - Attach CV + cover letter (if allowed) - Track in apply_tracker.py add --platform linkedin

Greenhouse (see hacking_ways/greenhouse_tips.md): - Manual form, repeat across companies - Pre-fill with browser autofill / extension (Bitwarden, Autofill) - Usually requires a few custom questions — answer once, save to knowledge/answers/

Lever (see hacking_ways/lever_tips.md): - Simplest flow — usually just CV + 1-2 questions - Often auto-acknowledges with confirmation email

Company site / Workday (see hacking_ways/workday_tips.md): - Most painful; pre-fill where possible - Often asks for the same info as your CV (work history) → keep a plain-text version ready

Referral: - Message your referrer BEFORE submitting - Share: role title, link, your tailored CV - They submit on their end → flag in tracker as --platform referral

Phase 4: Follow-up

Schedule

  • Day 0: Apply
  • Day 7: If no response → gentle follow-up email (if you have recruiter contact) OR LinkedIn message
  • Day 14: If still nothing → mark as ghosted in tracker, move on
  • Day 30: Re-engagement check — sometimes companies re-open roles

Phase 5: Interview Prep

Once you get an interview:

  1. Read the JD again — highlight the 3 must-have skills they'll definitely ask about
  2. Pull relevant experience bullets from your CV — prepare STAR-format stories
  3. Research the company: recent news, tech blog, engineering team on LinkedIn
  4. Mock-interview yourself or with a friend: behavioral + technical
  5. Prepare 2-3 questions to ask them (signal: you're evaluating them too)

STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result)

For each bullet on your CV, be able to expand into a 90-second STAR answer:

S: "At GoPay, the Crypto QA function was unstaffed." T: "I needed to build the entire function from zero in 11 months for CFX/Bappebti certification." A: "I established quality standards, wrote automation processes, owned ArgoCD-based release pipelines." R: "GoPay became one of only seven licensed crypto operators in Indonesia, delivered in 11 months vs multi-year industry norm."

Phase 6: Offer & Negotiation

When you get an offer:

  1. Don't accept immediately — say "thank you, I'll review and get back to you in X days"
  2. Compare with market: Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, Indonesian tech salary surveys
  3. Negotiate: total comp (base, bonus, equity, signing bonus), benefits, PTO, remote flexibility
  4. Once accepted: update tracker --status offer then --status withdrawn for other active apps

Weekly cadence

  • Mon: Review tracker stats — what's due for follow-up this week?
  • Tue-Thu: Apply to 3-5 high-quality roles
  • Fri: Update tracker, prepare for any upcoming interviews next week

Monthly cadence

  • Review companies_target.md — any new targets to add?
  • Review your CVs — anything to refresh based on new skills / projects?
  • Review your cover letters — any reusable answers worth saving?

Anti-patterns to avoid

  • ❌ Applying to 50 roles per day with the same CV (ATS picks up duplicates, low quality)
  • ❌ Skipping the JD parse step (you miss keyword matches)
  • ❌ Forgetting to track applications (you lose follow-up timing)
  • ❌ Ghosting recruiters who reach out (always respond, even to decline)
  • ❌ Not prepping for interviews because "you have many applications out"
  • ❌ Accepting the first offer without negotiation
